# Portfolio — Tone of Voice

**Audience:** top-of-funnel. Recruiters, hiring managers, and design leaders skimming fast.
**Goal of every line:** spark "I want to talk to this person."
**Personality in one line:** Warm and human, quietly confident. Sounds like a thoughtful designer explaining their work to a smart friend, not a press release.

## Principles

1. **Speak to user *and* business impact.** Every project answers: who did this help, and what did it move?
2. **Impact-first.** Lead with the result or the point; earn the read.
3. **Warm & human.** Approachable, a little personal. Confidence without trying hard.
4. **Mostly "I", generous with "we".** Own your decisions in first person; switch to "we" for genuinely shared wins. Collaborators get named in credits.
5. **Light craft vocabulary.** Plain by default. Use a precise design/product term only when it earns its place — never as filler or flex.
6. **Rhythm: punchy hook, flowing body.** Open with a sharp fragment, then warm, complete sentences.
7. **Humor only in hooks & problem framing.** Outcomes, metrics, captions, and credits stay earnest and credible.
8. **Never inflate.** If impact isn't quotable, keep it honest and qualitative. Confidence comes from clarity, not hype.

## Mechanics

- **No em dashes.** Use periods or commas.
- Short paragraphs, one idea each.
- Active voice. Concrete over abstract ("pull in live data" > "leverage dynamic data sources").
- Avoid buzzwords: leverage, synergy, delight, north star, passionate, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, game-changer, holistic, ideate, utilize.

## By section

| Section | Voice |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | Impact-first, one punchy line. Humor welcome. *"10+ data-driven templates, shipped to millions of Microsoft 365 users."* |
| **Problem** | Set the tension. Wry, human takes welcome. *"Templates were glorified blank pages, and the blank page scared people off."* |
| **Approach** | Punchy opening fragment, then flowing explanation of decisions and tradeoffs. First person. |
| **Outcome** | Earnest, specific, credible. Scale words ok ("millions of users"); no inflation. |
| **Captions** | Straightforward and informative. No jokes. |
| **Credits** | Plain and generous. |

## Litmus test

Before shipping a line: *Would I say this out loud to a design leader over coffee?* Does it speak to a real person's experience and a business result? If it reads like a deck or buzzword bingo, rewrite.
